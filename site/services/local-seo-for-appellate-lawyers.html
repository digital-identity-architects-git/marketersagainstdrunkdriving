What actually gets in the way

Before the tactics, the constraints. These are the three that shape every local SEO decision for appellate attorneys.

The referral source is other attorneys, not the public
Very low search volume in an extremely specialized niche
Trial firms see appellate counsel as a threat to the relationship
The objection we hear most: "Our referrals come from trial lawyers, not Google." It is a fair objection, and the program is built to answer it rather than talk around it.

How other lawyers, and clients who lost at trial search

Professional and procedural: "deadline to file notice of appeal [state]", "standard of review abuse of discretion", "appellate attorney for trial counsel".

Who buys
an appellate boutique
Decision cycle
Days to weeks against a hard notice-of-appeal deadline.
Deal value
Substantial flat or hourly fees on a briefing engagement.
What proves you
Published opinions, reversal record, and writing samples that speak for themselves.

What Local SEO includes

Scoped for appellate attorneys, not lifted from a generic retainer.

  • Google Business Profile setup, optimization and ongoing posting
  • Citation cleanup and NAP consistency across the directories that matter
  • Location pages built per market, not spun per zip code
  • Review generation systems that keep a steady, credible flow
  • Local rank tracking on a grid, not a single office pin

Questions Appellate Attorneys ask us

Should we market to lawyers or to clients?

To lawyers, primarily. The content that wins here is written for trial counsel — preservation of error, standard of review, when to bring in appellate counsel pre-verdict. That audience is small, findable, and worth a great deal.

Does that reassure or threaten the referring firm?

It reassures, if the framing is co-counsel rather than replacement. That framing is a deliberate editorial choice on every page.

Is the volume really there?

No, and it does not need to be. This is a practice where a dozen right readers a month is a business.
