What actually gets in the way

Before the tactics, the constraints. These are the three that shape every SEO decision for elder law attorneys.

The searcher is rarely the client — it is the daughter, not the parent
Medicaid rules are state-specific and change often
Competing with financial advisors selling the same planning outcome
The objection we hear most: "Financial advisors are eating this work." It is a fair objection, and the program is built to answer it rather than talk around it.

How adult children arranging care for aging parents search

Caregiver-shaped: "how to protect assets from nursing home", "Medicaid look-back period [state]", "how to get guardianship of a parent".

Who buys
an elder law and Medicaid-planning practice
Decision cycle
Weeks, triggered by a health crisis or a nursing-home admission.
Deal value
Four to five figures for planning engagements.
What proves you
Certification, state-specific rule fluency, and a tone that respects an exhausted caregiver.

What SEO includes

Scoped for elder law attorneys, not lifted from a generic retainer.

  • Keyword and intent map built around how buyers in your vertical actually search
  • Technical audit and fixes — crawl, speed, indexation, schema
  • Service and location page architecture that supports the whole practice
  • Internal linking that concentrates authority on the pages that earn revenue
  • Monthly reporting tied to leads, not vanity rank charts

Questions Elder Law Attorneys ask us

Who are we actually writing for?

The adult child, almost always a daughter in her fifties, researching at night after a hospital call. Content written to the parent misses the reader entirely — that single reframe usually doubles engagement.

How do we handle state-specific Medicaid rules?

One authoritative state page, reviewed on a schedule, with a visible review date. Generic national content does not convert here and can be wrong enough to be dangerous.

Is guardianship worth its own cluster?

Yes. It is urgent, non-optional, and much less contested than Medicaid planning.
