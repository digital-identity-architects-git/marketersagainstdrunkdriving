What actually gets in the way

Before the tactics, the constraints. These are the three that shape every content marketing decision for engineering firms.

Marketing is seen as beneath the profession and gets no budget
Selection is qualifications-based, which feels immune to search
Technical content is hard to write and easy to get embarrassingly wrong
The objection we hear most: "Selection is qualifications-based. Search does not matter." It is a fair objection, and the program is built to answer it rather than talk around it.

How developers, architects, contractors and public agencies search

Highly technical and problem-shaped: "structural engineer for foundation issue", "MEP design for [building type]", "geotechnical report requirements".

Who buys
a principal engineer or business development lead
Decision cycle
Months, relationship and qualification driven.
Deal value
Substantial multi-phase engagements.
What proves you
PE licensure, project type experience, and technical writing that demonstrates competence.

What Content Marketing includes

Scoped for engineering firms, not lifted from a generic retainer.

  • Topical map covering the full decision journey in your vertical
  • Editorial calendar with a publishing cadence you can actually sustain
  • Long-form pieces written by someone who understands the subject matter
  • Internal linking and content refresh cycles so old work keeps earning
  • Conversion paths built into the content, not bolted on after

Questions Engineering Firms ask us

Selection is QBS. Does search matter?

It matters before selection. Architects and owner reps look for specialist engineers by problem type constantly, and those searches are almost entirely unserved. A handful of technical pages can put you in rooms qualifications alone would not.

Who writes technical content credibly?

Your engineers, structured and edited by us. Ghost-written engineering content reads as fake to the only audience that matters.

Residential or commercial focus?

Residential structural inquiries have far more volume and shorter cycles; commercial has the value. Most firms should build both, starting with residential for velocity.
