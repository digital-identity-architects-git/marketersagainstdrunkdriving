Marketing for Criminal Defense Lawyers

Flat-fee or hourly defense on felony, misdemeanor and DUI matters.

Panicked and specific. They search the charge, not the practice area: "DWI first offense penalties", "possession with intent lawyer", plus the county.

The shape of the problem

Search volume spikes at nights and weekends when nobody is answering
Half the callers cannot afford private counsel and will end up with a public defender
Reviews are structurally hard — satisfied clients rarely want a public record of why they hired you
Who buys
a solo practitioner or small-firm partner who answers their own phone
Decision cycle
Hours. Bond hearings do not wait, and the family calls the first firm that answers.
Deal value
Flat fees from a few thousand to well into five figures on serious felonies.
What proves you
Case results stated within bar rules, former-prosecutor background, and clear, unembarrassed explanations of what happens next.
Compliance
Bar rules on result claims are strict, and client confidentiality shapes what can be published at all. Content is built to convert without naming anyone.

Questions Criminal Defense Lawyers ask us

How do we compete when clients will not review us?

You lean on the assets that do not require a client to out themselves: charge-specific content, county-level pages, video explaining process, and a review workflow that makes it easy for the ones who are willing. Volume matters less than recency and specificity.

Should we chase DUI or the whole practice?

DUI usually funds the rest. It has the highest volume and the shortest decision cycle, so we build that cluster first and use its authority to lift the felony pages.

What about after-hours calls?

We wire answering and speed-to-lead automation into the site, because a criminal defense lead that waits until Monday is already someone else client.
