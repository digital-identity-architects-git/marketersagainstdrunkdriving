Marketing for Farm Equipment Dealers

New and used equipment sales, parts, service and financing.

Model and problem specific: "[model] for sale", "[model] hydraulic problem", "combine parts near me".

Industries › Agriculture & Agribusiness › Farm Equipment Dealers

The shape of the problem

Manufacturer marketing controls the brand message and leaves dealers generic
Used inventory listings live on marketplaces the dealer does not own
Parts and service revenue is stable but almost never marketed
Who buys
the dealership owner or marketing manager
Decision cycle
Weeks to months, tied to the crop calendar and tax planning.
Deal value
Six figures for major equipment; recurring parts and service revenue.
What proves you
Live inventory, service capability, parts availability, and technician expertise.
Seasonality
Purchases cluster before planting and after harvest, with year-end tax buying.

Questions Farm Equipment Dealers ask us

The manufacturer markets for us. What is left?

Everything local: parts, service, used inventory and troubleshooting. Manufacturer campaigns build the brand; they do not make a producer in your county call your parts counter. Model-specific troubleshooting content is remarkably effective and almost nobody publishes it.

Is service worth marketing?

It is the most stable revenue you have and the least marketed. Ranking for "[model] repair near me" fills the shop in the off-season.

What about used inventory?

It should live on your own site with proper schema, not only on third-party marketplaces where you rent the audience.
