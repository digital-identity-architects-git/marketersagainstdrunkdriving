Marketing for Family Law Attorneys

Divorce, custody, support, modification and mediation representation.

Long, anonymous, question-shaped, and often at 2am: "how is custody decided in [state]", "can my ex move out of state with my kids", "how much does a divorce cost".

The shape of the problem

Prospects research for months anonymously and never fill out a form
Emotionally loaded queries where tone decides whether they call you or the next firm
Competing against DIY divorce services on the low end and big firms on the high end
Who buys
a partner at a family-law boutique
Decision cycle
Weeks to months of quiet research, then a fast decision once they commit.
Deal value
Retainers from a few thousand into the tens of thousands on contested custody.
What proves you
Plain-language explanations, a photo that looks like a person rather than a stock lawyer, and testimonials about how they were treated as much as what they won.
Compliance
Bar rules plus real discretion — no client detail, and content that never sensationalizes.

Questions Family Law Attorneys ask us

Our leads all come from referrals. Does search matter?

Referrals still Google you. A referred prospect checks your site and reviews before calling, and that check is where firms quietly lose warm leads. Search work protects the referral pipeline before it adds to it.

How do you handle such an emotional topic?

The content is written to reduce fear, not to sell. Practical, calm, specific to your state. That tone is the differentiator in this practice area — most competitors sound like a brochure.

Custody or divorce first?

Custody. It is higher intent, higher value, and the queries are more specific, which means less competition per query.
